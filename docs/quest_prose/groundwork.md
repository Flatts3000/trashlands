# Groundwork

## The Water Tank

You never fill this one. In the spreader it holds nothing and does not need sky. The Tree Nursery uses the same block later and does hold water.

You have made one already; it is inside your Rain Collector. The spreader needs its own.

## The Solar Panel

Caps the tower, and makes FE in direct daylight even once it is part of a spreader.

Build where that top block sees open sky if you want the power. The spreader itself runs on nothing.

## What the Pump Was For

Salvage had you pull one out of a washing machine. This is the machine it was for.

If you need another, that teardown also teaches an idea toward the Pump. Four make the blueprint, and after that you craft them.

## The Grass Spreader

Put the core down on ground you want green and it pulls the rest of the parts out of your inventory. With none there it waits while you stack them by hand: tank, pump, a copper pipe on each of the pump's four sides, panel on top. Sneak-place for a bare core and no assembly.

Formed, it turns coarse dirt to grass out to a radius and keeps going, consuming nothing. It skips ground that is roofed over or too dark.

Break any one part and the whole machine comes apart and hands back every piece.

## Mound Ground

The dark earth under a mound's footprint. Dark ground means that mound is regrowing toward the size it was.

Green it and it is finished. Erosion can take the grass off later without ever putting the dark back.

So pick your mounds. One that refills keeps giving you garbage; a retired one gives you what the land grows.

## The Compost Heap

Organic muck and fiber scrap are the staples, but it takes most plant matter: leaves, saplings, flowers, crops, seeds, mushrooms. Once a layer has finished, right-click it and the compost turns out of the face you clicked.

Everything after this runs on compost.

## The Frontier

Soil bordering the dump reverts, one ring at a time, and only while you are near it. Every kind of dirt goes, grass included, and it makes no difference who put it there. Interior ground holds until the edge reaches it.

An unquarried mound, a trash bag or a bale reverts the ground beside it the same way coarse dirt does.

Buildings are safe. Only soil reverts.

Inside the spreader's radius the machine re-greens what reverted. Ground is lost at the rim.

## Fertilizer

On reclaimed grass it scatters plants across about the patch vanilla bone meal covers. It does nothing on coarse dirt and is not spent there, so grass comes first.

On mycelium it puts up mushrooms instead.

Anywhere else it works as plain bone meal. Nothing on the sprawl drops bones, so until the demolition yard this is the only way to hurry a crop or a sapling.

## The Unknown Seedling

Something comes up in a finished compost layer now and then and turns out along with the fertilizer.

No recipe makes one and nothing in the world drops one. More compost is the only way to more seedlings.

## Farmland

No hoe exists here and tilling is switched off, so farmland is crafted rather than dug.

Fertilizer and dirt make it. Coarse dirt works too and costs four times the fertilizer, so breaking a patch of your own reclaimed grass for dirt is cheaper than converting dead ground.

## The Tree Nursery

Another multiblock, and a wider one: the core, a Water Tank beside it, and two Solar Panels across the top. The spreader already took a tank and a panel, so budget a second tank and two more panels before you start this.

Water, Fertilizer and a seedling in. Pick a species on its screen; one sapling comes out after a long cook. A pipe can keep the water topped up, but fertilizer, seedlings and the sapling itself are hand-only, and pulling an input mid-cook drops the progress back to nothing.

Nothing in this world drops a sapling, including a tree you cut down yourself, so this is where they come from.

Grow oak at some point. Apples come off oak and dark oak leaves and nowhere else.

## Cover

A plant on the border is stripped instead of the soil under it. You lose the plant and keep the ground.

A border you are holding goes bare before it goes brown, which is your warning. Fertilizing it again puts the cover back.

## Keep It Wet

Wet farmland is left alone. Dry farmland reverts like any other soil, and the crop on it drops on the floor rather than being destroyed, so you lose the plot and the growth but keep the seed.

Put the plot inside the spreader's radius; it spares moist farmland the same way it re-greens grass.

## Trees Hold It

Plant the sapling on grass and leave it room to grow.

Ground near logs and leaves stops reverting. Grass stays contested and cover absorbs one hit, so a line of trees along the border is the only permanent hold.

## A Crafting Table

The vanilla one.

Cut what you grew and build it. A felled tree drops no sapling, so the next one comes out of the nursery.

## Herbivore Bait

A cat, a wolf or a pigeon turns up out here occasionally. Nothing you can farm spawns on its own.

This one takes an apple and two wheat, so it needs both halves of the chapter: oak for the apple, and a seedling that came up wheat for the rest.

The seedling is also what the nursery wants. Wheat and trees compete.

## Leave It Alone

Set it on grass and walk away. It settles only while no player is near, then spawns one animal and is spent.

Walking back over resets its progress to zero rather than pausing it.

In a cluster only one settles at a time; the others hold without losing progress and take their turn as each fires.

Not on grass, nothing happens.

## A Breeding Pair

Two of the same bait make the rich version, and it puts down an adult and its young instead of one animal.

A single animal is a dead end. From a pair, ordinary breeding takes over.

## The Other Two Diets

Carnivore bait brings cats, ocelots, foxes and armadillos. Omnivore brings chickens, pigs and parrots.

Carnivore takes two apples and raw meat, and the only meat here walks, so grazers come first. Omnivore is one of each of the other two, so three apples and everything that went into both.
