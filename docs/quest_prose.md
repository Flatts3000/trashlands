## Welcome

### Welcome to Trashlands

A coarse dirt plain covered in mounds of garbage. No ore under it, no trees on it.

Everything you build starts as a Block of Garbage you dug out of a mound.

The Salvager's Manual comes with this quest. It goes deeper than this book does.

### Join the Discord

The numbers in this pack are first-pass. Bug reports and balance complaints are both useful.

Click the Discord badge above. It opens the invite in your browser.

### The Book Is Not Finished

This pack is an alpha, and the quest book is the least finished part of it.

What is here is a rough guide, not the shape it will take. Most of the work is going into Recompile, the mod this pack is built on, and the book gets rewritten once that settles.

The Salvager's Manual covers more. Use it first.

### What Spawns Here

Nothing hostile spawns on the dump, so you do not need a shelter. A few friendly mobs turn up, rarely, and none of them are food.

Sleep anyway. Phantoms still come after a few nights without it.

The demolition yard is different. Things spawn there in numbers, so bring a weapon.

### Water

There is no water in this world at first. Rain is the only source early on, and the Rain Collector catches it.

Two source blocks will not fill a third. That vanilla trick is off here, so water never multiplies.

There is no thirst bar, so going without costs you nothing until you build something that drinks.

### Pick Through the Garbage

Hold right-click on a Block of Garbage where it stands. It gives one item at a time, and after two or three the block is gone.

Sorting where it stands takes no tool, whatever you are holding. Breaking a block to carry it needs a shovel, and without one you get nothing. Trash Bags are the exception: they break by hand.

What comes out is random, so this takes a few blocks.

### Green Ground Erodes

Grass does not spread here, and coarse dirt reverts it at the edge. Green ground erodes inward.

Only soil touching unhealed ground goes. The interior holds until the edge reaches it, blocks you placed are never touched, and none of it runs while you are logged off.

This is a long way off. It matters once you start reclaiming ground, and the machines that heal it also hold it.


## Salvage

### Scrap Knife

Another one you can make in your inventory grid.

It opens sealed Tin Cans, and it is the only thing that picks up or sorts a Compacted Bale.

### The Scrap Crafting Table

There is no wood in this world, so there is no vanilla crafting table.

This one replaces it, and it fits the 2x2 grid in your inventory.

Put it down somewhere you have room to build around. Scrap blocks touching each other are one Scrap Network, and the panel down the right of its screen lists what the whole network is holding.

### Prybar

Small enough to make in your inventory grid. It opens Bulky Waste, and nothing else does.

### Something to Eat

Nothing spawns in the garbage biome, so there is nothing to hunt.

A sealed can is not food yet. Hold the can and right-click with a Scrap Knife anywhere in your inventory. You do not have to hold the knife, and it is not used up.

Dump Mushrooms grow on the mycelium between mounds and place back down, so you can replant a patch.

### The Sorting Tarp

Right-click the tarp with a Block of Garbage, a Trash Bag, or a Compacted Bale in hand and it sifts that one straight out. Hold right-click to keep feeding it.

A Trash Bag breaks by hand. A Block of Garbage needs a shovel to carry here. A Compacted Bale needs a Scrap Knife to pick up at all.

Nothing crumbles in your hands this way. It has no screen and stores nothing, so everything lands on the ground around it.

A Scrap Barrel touching the tarp catches all of it instead.

### The Burn Barrel

The first smelter in this world. It opens the ordinary furnace screen.

It burns scrap metal, food, and a few other odds of refuse. Other metals will not go in it. Pipes and hoppers cannot reach it, so load it by hand.

Stand a Scrap Barrel against it and finished smelts move across as they come.

It needs fuel. There is no coal and no wood in this world.

### The Recompile Workbench

Rack both tools on it by right-clicking the bench with each in turn. It has a slot for a Scrap Knife and a slot for a Prybar, and different finds take different tools.

Then hold right-click with whatever you want taken apart.

The racked tool wears down as it works. When it breaks, nothing comes apart until you rack another.

What comes off lands on top of the bench. Stand the bench against a Scrap Barrel and it goes into the barrel instead.

### Open Bulky Waste

Right-click it with a Prybar. It opens in one action, drops a single find, and breaks. Mining it by hand gives nothing.

Most finds are ordinary. About one in ten comes from a better pool.

### Junk Shovel

A Block of Garbage or a pile of Stone Rubble breaks to nothing without a shovel in hand. Any shovel will do.

It digs the coarse dirt faster too.

### Fuel

Made from two things you are already pulling out of the garbage.

One rag burns as long as charcoal: eight items. Junk burns at two.

### The Pump

Find a Washing Machine in Bulky Waste and tear it down at the Workbench with a Prybar racked. It gives one Pump, plus scrap and plastic.

The teardown also gives an idea toward the Pump. Four make a blueprint, and after that you craft them.

It is a component of the Grass Spreader, the machine that turns coarse dirt back to grass.

### Dirty Mattress

The commonest thing in Bulky Waste, and the only bed until you can make a clean one.

Sleeping on it breaks it when you get up. Sneak-right-click to set spawn instead; that does not wear it out.

Tearing one down at the Workbench with a Scrap Knife gives an idea toward the Clean Mattress. Four and you can make the clean one.

### Scrap Barrel

Every vanilla container needs wood, so this is the first storage this world has.

It opens the ordinary chest screen and holds a chest's worth of anything.

It is also the first block that can catch what the others put out. Scrap blocks touching face to face are one Scrap Network, with no pipes or wires. Set the barrel against the Sorting Tarp and sifted materials land in it rather than on the floor. The Burn Barrel and the Recompile Workbench feed it the same way.

Touching means sharing a face. A diagonal is a separate network, and so is a gap.

### Copper

Burn Scrap Metal in the barrel and it comes back as copper nuggets, one for one.

Spend them on Copper Pipes; the Rain Collector takes one. Do not press them into ingots, because nothing you can build yet uses one.

Copper is what this world runs on. Iron is out past the demolition yard, behind a furnace you cannot build yet.

### The Rain Collector

Set the collector down, then stack the funnel on top of it. The collector on its own will hold water but gather none.

It needs open sky above the funnel. Under a roof it stays empty.

It fills only while rain is falling, about a minute from empty to full. Full is four buckets.

Right-click with a glass bottle or a bucket to draw water off. Breaking it keeps the water, so you can move it.

### A Bucket of Water

There is no recipe for a bucket; vanilla's is switched off. They come out of household garbage, so keep sorting.

Fill it at the Rain Collector.


## The Depths

### Slag

There is no recipe for slag. The Cupola rakes a lump into its fourth slot every eighth smelt.

Counted, not rolled. If that slot is full the count carries until there is room.

### Or: Concrete Powder

The Separator splits a lump into gray concrete powder, with scrap metal as the byproduct. Worth knowing before every lump goes to the portal.

### The Slag Furnace

Burns fuel, not power. Charcoal goes in the bottom slot; coal does not exist yet.

One lump takes twice as long as an ordinary smelt.

### The Only Obsidian

One lump, one block. Nothing else here makes obsidian, and each lump cost eight Cupola smelts.

A portal needs ten blocks, or fourteen if you fill the corners.

### Techno-Organic Waste

Right-click to pick through it, the same as a mound up top. The depths are solid from bedrock floor to bedrock ceiling, so this is most of what is down here.

It does not fall.

### Lignite

Brown coal, the rank below the coal vanilla ships.

It burns on its own at half a coal, so it is useful before you upgrade it.

### Ancient Sculk

Seams run through the fill, lit from inside.

A diamond sledgehammer, or netherite. Copper and iron leave nothing behind however long you swing. The diamond one wants a whole diamond block, so this is gem-tier work however early you find a seam.

### Nether Wart

Separate Rendered Organics for it. Wart grows on soul sand and nothing else, which the rubble already gives you.

### Slag Rubble

This one does fall. Sort it rather than stack it.

### Netherite Scrap

Out of the waste, uncommonly. There is no ancient debris to mine for, though a bastion chest may hold some.

### Terrain You Craft

The rock generates no vanilla nether block. Netherrack, basalt, blackstone and both nyliums come out of slag rubble as shards, four to a block.

Fortresses and bastions still generate and still hold what they always did, so you can find some there. You cannot mine for them.

### Soul Sand

Soul sand and soul soil arrive as Clumps, not shards. Search JEI for the clump.

### Nylium, and Fungus Forests

Bone meal on nylium grows a fungus. Bone meal on that fungus, standing on its own nylium, grows a huge one.

Nothing down here seeds a forest on its own, so these two shards are the only way to start one. A forest is renewable stems and hyphae.

### Coal, At Last

Smelt the lignite in the Burn Barrel. You do not need the Cupola.

One lignite spent as fuel smelts four more.

This is the only coal in the world.

### A Catalyst Needs a Shard

Three to five powder a block, never the block itself. One packs back into sculk, two make four veins.

Eight around redstone is a sensor, around soul sand a shrieker. Around an echo shard, the catalyst. The only shard in this world sits in a sewer sump, back up in the yard.

### A Blaze Rod, Without a Fortress

Press four blaze powder into a briquette at any bench, then fire the briquette in the Sintering Kiln.

Four powder in, and a rod breaks back into two, so do not round-trip them.

This is a brewing stand without a fortress.

### The Worn Forging Die

These come out of the waste about six times as often as netherite scrap.

Tear one down at the Workbench with a Prybar. It gives back almost nothing, but each one teaches a quarter of the netherite upgrade. Four and you have the pattern.

That blueprint is the only smithing template you can make here.


## Groundwork

### The Water Tank

You never fill this one. In the spreader it holds nothing and does not need sky. The Tree Nursery uses the same block later and does hold water.

You have made one already; it is inside your Rain Collector. The spreader needs its own.

### The Solar Panel

Caps the tower, and makes FE in direct daylight even once it is part of a spreader.

Build where that top block sees open sky if you want the power. The spreader itself runs on nothing.

### What the Pump Was For

Salvage had you pull one out of a washing machine. This is the machine it was for.

If you need another, that teardown also teaches an idea toward the Pump. Four make the blueprint, and after that you craft them.

### The Grass Spreader

Put the core down on ground you want green and it pulls the rest of the parts out of your inventory. With none there it waits while you stack them by hand: tank, pump, a copper pipe on each of the pump's four sides, panel on top. Sneak-place for a bare core and no assembly.

Formed, it turns coarse dirt to grass out to a radius and keeps going, consuming nothing. It skips ground that is roofed over or too dark.

Break any one part and the whole machine comes apart and hands back every piece.

### Mound Ground

The dark earth under a mound's footprint. Dark ground means that mound is regrowing toward the size it was.

Green it and it is finished. Erosion can take the grass off later without ever putting the dark back.

So pick your mounds. One that refills keeps giving you garbage; a retired one gives you what the land grows.

### The Compost Heap

Organic muck and fiber scrap are the staples, but it takes most plant matter: leaves, saplings, flowers, crops, seeds, mushrooms. Once a layer has finished, right-click it and the compost turns out of the face you clicked.

Everything after this runs on compost.

### The Frontier

Soil bordering the dump reverts, one ring at a time, and only while you are near it. Every kind of dirt goes, grass included, and it makes no difference who put it there. Interior ground holds until the edge reaches it.

An unquarried mound, a trash bag or a bale reverts the ground beside it the same way coarse dirt does.

Buildings are safe. Only soil reverts.

Inside the spreader's radius the machine re-greens what reverted. Ground is lost at the rim.

### Fertilizer

On reclaimed grass it scatters plants across about the patch vanilla bone meal covers. It does nothing on coarse dirt and is not spent there, so grass comes first.

On mycelium it puts up mushrooms instead.

Anywhere else it works as plain bone meal. Nothing on the sprawl drops bones, so until the demolition yard this is the only way to hurry a crop or a sapling.

### The Unknown Seedling

Something comes up in a finished compost layer now and then and turns out along with the fertilizer.

No recipe makes one and nothing in the world drops one. More compost is the only way to more seedlings.

### Farmland

No hoe exists here and tilling is switched off, so farmland is crafted rather than dug.

Fertilizer and dirt make it. Coarse dirt works too and costs four times the fertilizer, so breaking a patch of your own reclaimed grass for dirt is cheaper than converting dead ground.

### The Tree Nursery

Another multiblock, and a wider one: the core, a Water Tank beside it, and two Solar Panels across the top. The spreader already took a tank and a panel, so budget a second tank and two more panels before you start this.

Water, Fertilizer and a seedling in. Pick a species on its screen; one sapling comes out after a long cook. A pipe can keep the water topped up, but fertilizer, seedlings and the sapling itself are hand-only, and pulling an input mid-cook drops the progress back to nothing.

Nothing in this world drops a sapling, including a tree you cut down yourself, so this is where they come from.

Grow oak at some point. Apples come off oak and dark oak leaves and nowhere else.

### Cover

A plant on the border is stripped instead of the soil under it. You lose the plant and keep the ground.

A border you are holding goes bare before it goes brown, which is your warning. Fertilizing it again puts the cover back.

### Keep It Wet

Wet farmland is left alone. Dry farmland reverts like any other soil, and the crop on it drops on the floor rather than being destroyed, so you lose the plot and the growth but keep the seed.

Put the plot inside the spreader's radius; it spares moist farmland the same way it re-greens grass.

### Trees Hold It

Plant the sapling on grass and leave it room to grow.

Ground near logs and leaves stops reverting. Grass stays contested and cover absorbs one hit, so a line of trees along the border is the only permanent hold.

### A Crafting Table

The vanilla one.

Cut what you grew and build it. A felled tree drops no sapling, so the next one comes out of the nursery.

### Herbivore Bait

A cat, a wolf or a pigeon turns up out here occasionally. Nothing you can farm spawns on its own.

This one takes an apple and two wheat, so it needs both halves of the chapter: oak for the apple, and a seedling that came up wheat for the rest.

The seedling is also what the nursery wants. Wheat and trees compete.

### Leave It Alone

Set it on grass and walk away. It settles only while no player is near, then spawns one animal and is spent.

Walking back over resets its progress to zero rather than pausing it.

In a cluster only one settles at a time; the others hold without losing progress and take their turn as each fires.

Not on grass, nothing happens.

### A Breeding Pair

Two of the same bait make the rich version, and it puts down an adult and its young instead of one animal.

A single animal is a dead end. From a pair, ordinary breeding takes over.

### The Other Two Diets

Carnivore bait brings cats, ocelots, foxes and armadillos. Omnivore brings chickens, pigs and parrots.

Carnivore takes two apples and raw meat, and the only meat here walks, so grazers come first. Omnivore is one of each of the other two, so three apples and everything that went into both.
